\subsection{Follow-up results}
\label{sec:followup-results}

All 1,580 follow-up tasks completed without failure or divergence. One labelling bug in the
analysis preparer (standard carrier runs labelled as colour-augmented) halted the registered
script before it produced any output; it was fixed and recorded as Amendment~9 before any F3
or F4 estimate was seen. Table~\ref{tab:followup} gives the eight registered tests.

\caption{Where the leaked signal lives. Left: leak minus clean accuracy of the 160 Study~2 2C
models under each test-time ablation (mean and 95\% $t$ interval over 80
architecture--seed--diversity pairs); stain transfer (F2) highlighted. Middle: the same contrast
for models trained on fresh seeds under the standard recipe, with every background masked
(F3), or with HED colour augmentation (F4). Right: CRIC, within-cell P1 $-$ P2 accuracy for
cells whose class agrees or disagrees with their slide's majority, and the context-class
probability for disagreeing cells (F1); slide-bootstrap intervals.}
\label{fig:carriers}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{The leak needs no background (F3).} Trained and tested with every background replaced
by a constant, leak models beat clean models by \FthreeEst~pp (\FthreeLo--\FthreeHi; Holm
$p$~\FthreePholm), at least as much as under the standard recipe on the same fresh seeds
(\CarrierStandard~pp, which also replicates H3: \HthreeRepEst~pp, \HthreeRepLo--\HthreeRepHi).
At test time, removing the background from the original 2C models' inputs likewise left most of
the advantage (\LeakUnderBg~pp of \LeakUnderOriginal; Figure~\ref{fig:carriers}).

\paragraph{The leak is tied to the field's colour rendering at test time (F2), but colour
augmentation does not prevent it (F4).} Moving each test cell's LAB colour statistics to those
of an unseen field of the same class cut the leak advantage from \LeakUnderOriginal\ to
\LeakUnderStain~pp, a reduction of \FtwoEst~pp (\FtwoLo--\FtwoHi; Holm $p=\FtwoPholm$). The
other interventions with comparable accuracy costs did not: with illumination flattened the
advantage was \LeakUnderFlat~pp and with the background removed \LeakUnderBg~pp; greyscale and
low-pass filtering degraded accuracy too far to be informative. Yet models trained with strong
HED colour augmentation leaked as much as standard models (\CarrierColour\ versus
\CarrierStandard~pp; F4 reduction \FfourEst, \FfourLo\ to \FfourHi).

\paragraph{Exploratory: not shared pixels.} In leak cells a third or more of the test crops
overlap a training sibling in the source image. The leak advantage was the same on crops that
share no pixel with any training crop (\OverlapNo~pp) as on crops that do (\OverlapYes~pp), so
the leak generalises to new cells from a seen field rather than reflecting memorised pixels;
stain transfer removed it in both subsets (\OverlapStainNo\ and \OverlapStainYes~pp). This split
was not registered.

\paragraph{No slide-level label prior (F1).} CRIC cells whose class disagreed with the majority
class of their slide received \emph{less}, not more, probability on that majority class when
their slide was in training (\FoneEst~pp, \FoneLo\ to \FoneHi, over \FoneCells\ cells from
\FoneSlides\ slides; F1 not supported). Crop-level training raised accuracy as much for these
cells (\FoneDiscordant~pp) as for cells agreeing with their slide (\FoneConcordant~pp; contrast
\FoneContrast~pp, \FoneContrastLo\ to \FoneContrastHi): seen slides help each cell toward its own
class, not toward the slide's typical class.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/dose.pdf}
\caption{The P1 $-$ P2 gap against within-class parent recoverability $R$ along three
manipulations (grey: architectures; colour: pooled over architectures that passed the
manipulation check, at levels they share; only registered in-regime levels are shown). Left:
rank-limited LEACE erasure, ranks 0--256, twelve architectures. Middle: Reinhard normalisation
strength 0--1, four architectures (all failed the manipulation check and are excluded from F8).
Right: probe-matched linear adversary, three architectures.}
\label{fig:dose}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{The gap does not track the fingerprint (F5--F8).} Three manipulations lowered $R$ by
different routes, and none moved the gap (Figure~\ref{fig:dose}). Rank-256 LEACE erasure cut
recoverability from \LeaceRzero\% to \LeaceRtop\% (the class-only floor is \RFloor\%) in all twelve
architectures while costing P2 \LeaceAccDrop~pp, and the gap stayed at \LeaceGapZero\ to
\LeaceGapTop~pp (F5 \FfiveEst, \FfiveLo\ to \FfiveHi). The probe-matched linear adversary passed
its manipulation check in all three architectures, lowering $R$ from \LinRzeroMin--\LinRzeroMax\%
to \LinRstarMin--\LinRstarMax\% inside the regime, with a slope of gap on $R$ of \FsevenEst\
(\FsevenLo\ to \FsevenHi). Stain normalisation lowered $R$ only from \NormRzero\% to \NormRtop\%
while costing P2 \NormAccDrop~pp, failed its manipulation check for every architecture, and left
the gap unchanged (F6 \FsixEst~pp per unit strength, \FsixLo\ to \FsixHi). Pooled over the
\FeightCells\ included manipulation--architecture cells, the slope was \FeightEst\
(\FeightLo\ to \FeightHi): even a 20-pp loss of recoverability would move the gap by at most
about 0.1~pp. The device probe used for $R$ agreed with the registered sklearn probe on the 280
Study~2 adversarial runs ($r=\ProbeAgreement$).

\paragraph{Grad-CAM sees no difference (descriptive).} Leak and clean models placed the same
share of Grad-CAM attribution outside the cell mask (\CamBgAttribution\% on average, against a
background pixel share of \CamBgPixels\%; leak minus clean \CamLeakEst~pp, \CamLeakLo\ to
\CamLeakHi). The maps depended on the trained head (median Spearman correlation \CamSpearman\
with a random head), so the absence of a difference is not a failure of the method's sanity
check; it is the expected result if the leaked signal lies in how the cell is rendered rather
than in where the model looks.
