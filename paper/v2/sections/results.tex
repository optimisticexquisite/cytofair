\subsection{Crop-level splitting inflates SIPaKMeD accuracy (H1)}
\label{sec:res-h1}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/h1.pdf}
\caption{Left: P1 $-$ P2 accuracy per architecture (mean and 95\% $t$ interval over 20 seeds)
and the registered pooled estimate (two-way cluster bootstrap interval). Right: the same for
P2 $-$ P3, a registered secondary contrast.}
\label{fig:h1}
\end{figure}

Mean test accuracy was \SipPOneAcc\% under crop-level splitting (P1) and \SipPTwoAcc\% under
parent-level splitting (P2). The registered estimate of the difference is \HoneEst~pp (95\%
cluster-bootstrap interval \HoneBootLo--\HoneBootHi, $p$~\HoneBootP; Figure~\ref{fig:h1}). The
mixed model gives the same estimate (Satterthwaite $df=\HoneDf$, $p$~\HoneKRp\ with
Kenward--Roger degrees of freedom), as do the seed-level sign-flip test ($p$~\HoneSignFlipP)
and the binomial mixed model (log odds ratio \HoneGlmmLogOR, $p$~\HoneGlmmP). All
\HonePositiveArchs\ architectures scored higher under P1, by \HoneArchMin--\HoneArchMax~pp, and
\HoneArchCIPositive\ of them individually had intervals excluding zero. Crop-level splitting
therefore hides about a quarter of the errors: the parent-level error rate is
\SipErrorRatio\ times the crop-level one. Macro-F1 (\HoneFoneEst~pp) and balanced accuracy
(\HoneBaccEst~pp) moved by the same amount.

Merging near-duplicate-linked parents on top of parent-level splitting changed nothing (P2
$-$ P3: \PtwoPthreeEst~pp, \PtwoPthreeLo\ to \PtwoPthreeHi, $p=\PtwoPthreeP$), so the inflation
comes from shared parents, not from the residual duplicates that cross parent boundaries. The
architecture ranking was largely preserved (Kendall $\tau=\KendallTau$; \ReorderedPairs\ pairs
reordered). Within crops tested under both protocols in different seeds (\MoneCrops\ crops),
accuracy was \MoneEst~pp higher under P1 (\MoneLo--\MoneHi). Within P1, test crops that shared
source pixels with a training crop were more often correct (log odds \MtwoOverlapLogOdds,
$p$~\MtwoOverlapP), as were crops with more training siblings (log odds \MtwoSiblingsLogOdds\
per log sibling, $p=\MtwoSiblingsP$).

\subsection{The CRIC inflation is an order of magnitude larger (H2)}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.72\linewidth]{figures/cric.pdf}
\caption{CRIC P1 $-$ P2 accuracy per architecture (mean and 95\% $t$ interval over ten seeds),
the registered CRIC estimate, and the SIPaKMeD estimate for scale.}
\label{fig:cric}
\end{figure}

CRIC accuracy fell from \CricPOneAcc\% under crop-level splitting to \CricPTwoAcc\% under
slide-level splitting: H2 $=\HtwoEst$~pp (\HtwoLo--\HtwoHi; Holm $p$~\HtwoPholm;
Figure~\ref{fig:cric}). The error rate rose by a factor of \CricErrorRatio. Because the CRIC
classes are imbalanced, balanced accuracy fell further, from \CricPOneBacc\% to
\CricPTwoBacc\% (\CricBaccEst~pp), and macro-F1 by \CricFoneEst~pp. The size-matched control
P1-prime gave the same inflation as P1 (\CricPrimeEst~pp), so the gap is not a training-size
effect. All six architectures showed gaps of similar size.

\subsection{The inflation is leakage, not training diversity (H3)}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/factorial.pdf}
\caption{The 2C factorial. Left: leak minus clean accuracy at matched crop and field counts,
per architecture and diversity level (mean and 95\% $t$ interval over ten seeds), and the
registered H3 estimate. Right: high minus low diversity within clean cells.}
\label{fig:factorial}
\end{figure}

With crop count, field count, and allocation rule held equal, adding the test fields' sibling
crops to the training set raised accuracy on the common test set by \HthreeEst~pp
(\HthreeLo--\HthreeHi; Holm $p=\HthreePholm$; Figure~\ref{fig:factorial}). Every architecture
showed the effect at both diversity levels. Increasing the number of distinct training fields
by a factor of 1.43 without leakage did nothing (\DivEst~pp, \DivLo\ to \DivHi), and the
leakage effect did not depend on diversity (interaction \InteractionEst~pp, $p=\InteractionP$).
The approximate decomposition registered for the natural splits accordingly assigns the
Phase~1 gap entirely to leakage (diversity component \DecompDiv~pp).

\subsection{Background transplants do not isolate the route (H4)}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/transplant.pdf}
\caption{Left: accuracy of leak and clean 2C models on composites of their common test set
(mean and 95\% $t$ interval over 80 runs each). Right: the per-run own-context route,
sibling minus unseen same-class background, and the registered H4 contrast.}
\label{fig:transplant}
\end{figure}

Compositing a cell onto its own inpainted background cost nothing, so seam and inpainting
artifacts were negligible (Figure~\ref{fig:transplant}). Leak models were \LeakOriginalEst~pp
more accurate than clean models on unmodified test cells and kept most of that advantage
when the background was replaced: \TransplantLeakSibling~pp on sibling backgrounds,
\TransplantLeakUnseen~pp on backgrounds from unseen fields of the same class, and
\TransplantLeakOther~pp on backgrounds of other classes. The registered own-context route was
\HfourEst~pp larger in leak models (\HfourLo\ to \HfourHi; Holm $p=\HfourPholm$), not
supported; its point estimate would assign \RouteShare\% of the inflation to the background.
Two observations qualify the design. Sibling backgrounds helped even clean models, which had
never seen those fields, by \TransplantCleanHarmony~pp, presumably because a background from
the cell's own field matches its stain; this effect is common to both arms and inflates the
variance of the contrast. And backgrounds do carry class information: an other-class
background moved \DonorShiftEst~pp of probability toward its class (\DonorShiftLo--\DonorShiftHi,
secondary).

\subsection{Gradient reversal was evaded (H5)}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/grl.pdf}
\caption{The gradient-reversal sweep. Left: within-class parent recoverability of the frozen
features on the probe split. Middle: P1 $-$ P2 accuracy (mean and 95\% $t$ interval over ten
seeds). Right: per-seed gap against recoverability; hollow grey points are excluded from H5
(ConvNeXt-T failed the registered manipulation check).}
\label{fig:grl}
\end{figure}

For ConvNeXt-T the adversary was evaded: as the reversal strength grew, an independent linear
probe identified the field \emph{better} (within-class recoverability \GrlConvRzero\% at
$\lambda=0$, \GrlConvRstar\% at $\lambda=3$), so this architecture failed the registered
manipulation check and was excluded (Figure~\ref{fig:grl}). For ResNet-50 recoverability fell
from \GrlResRzero\% to \GrlResRstar\%, passing the check, and the mean gap fell from
\GrlResGapZero~pp at $\lambda=0$ to between \GrlResGapMin\ and \GrlResGapMax~pp at every
$\lambda>0$, while P2 accuracy stayed within 1~pp. The registered slope of gap on recoverability
was positive but not significant (\HfiveEst~pp per pp, \HfiveLo\ to \HfiveHi; Holm
$p=\HfivePholm$).
